# Royal Caribbean International — Reference Brief for GCC Travel Agents

**Research date:** 6 July 2026.
**Source policy:** Royal Caribbean official websites only (royalcaribbean.com and official regional variants, e.g. royalcaribbean.com/aus/en, royalcaribbean.com/gbr/en, royalcaribbean.com/sgp/en). Claims not verifiable on official RC pages are marked **UNVERIFIED**. No third-party press, blogs, fan sites or OTAs were used. No prices or exact departure dates are included; only general season months as stated on official pages.

## 1. Fleet & ships

Royal Caribbean's own pages give **inconsistent total fleet counts** depending on page/snapshot — this is flagged rather than resolved by guesswork:
- The ship-listing page states the fleet is organised across all classes, oldest ship Grandeur of the Seas (1996) to newest Icon Class ships (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/guides/ships-by-age).
- The official FAQ "How Many Cruise Ships Does Royal Caribbean Have?" states the fleet totals **31 ships** (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/how-many-ships-are-in-royal-caribbeans-fleet).
- The official About Us page states the fleet is **30 ships**, with more than 50,000 crew ("Gold Anchor Society" language not used) delivering cruises to "more than 240 destinations" for "100 million+ guests" served since the brand's 1969 launch (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us).
- **UNVERIFIED**: the precise current total ship count — official pages give 30 vs 31 depending on page; agents should treat "around 30 ships" as accurate and confirm exact current count at time of booking.

### Icon Class (newest/flagship class)
Per the official cruise-ships overview, Icon Class is described as "the best family vacation in the world on four record smashing ships" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships):
- **Icon of the Seas** — launched 2024 (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us). Sails 7-night Eastern/Western Caribbean itineraries from Miami including Perfect Day at CocoCay (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas). Onboard features include Category 6 (billed as the largest waterpark at sea, with six record-breaking slides including the tallest waterslide at sea), Crown's Edge (a skywalk/ropes-course/thrill-ride hybrid), the AquaDome entertainment venue, and seven pools including the first suspended infinity pool at sea (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas). The ship has 28 room/suite categories (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/guides/icon-of-the-seas-room-types).
- **Star of the Seas** — launched 2025 (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us), sailing from Port Canaveral, Florida on Eastern/Western Caribbean itineraries (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/star-of-the-seas). Marketed as "The World's Best Family Vacation," featuring the largest waterpark at sea (six record-breaking waterslides), seven pools and ten whirlpools, 17 complimentary dining venues (40+ ways to dine/drink total across 23 cuisines), and 2,805 staterooms/suites across 28 categories (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/star-of-the-seas).
- **Legend of the Seas** — officially confirmed as the third Icon Class ship, arriving **Summer 2026**. Per its official ship page, it will sail Mediterranean itineraries in summer (from Barcelona, Spain and Rome/Civitavecchia, Italy) and Caribbean itineraries in winter (from Fort Lauderdale, Florida, Western/Southern Caribbean including Perfect Day at CocoCay) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas). Features include Category 6 waterpark, seven pools/ten whirlpools, eight neighbourhoods (AquaDome, Central Park, Thrill Island, Chill Island, Surfside, Royal Promenade, The Hideaway, Suite Neighbourhood), an Absolute Zero ice-skating arena, and 40+ dining/drink venues (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas).
- **Hero of the Seas** — officially confirmed as the fourth Icon Class ship, arriving **August 2027**, homeporting in Miami, Florida, sailing Eastern/Western Caribbean itineraries including Perfect Day at CocoCay. Features include nine swimming pools, 28 dining venues, and family cabin configurations including an "Ultimate Family Treehouse" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/hero-of-the-seas).
- **UNVERIFIED**: any fifth Icon Class ship beyond Hero of the Seas — no official royalcaribbean.com page confirming a further newbuild was found during this research (only non-official trade sources referenced a possible 2028 fifth ship; per the strict source policy, this is not reported as fact).

## 2. Worldwide 2026/27 deployment by region

All itinerary specifics below are as stated on official RC region/itinerary pages; night counts and ports are examples of "representative" sailings, not exhaustive.

### Caribbean (incl. Perfect Day at CocoCay)
- **Home ports:** Miami and Port Canaveral (Icon Class ships), Fort Lauderdale (Legend of the Seas, winter) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/star-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas).
- **Representative itineraries:** 7-night Eastern Caribbean & Perfect Day from Miami on Icon of the Seas; 7-night Eastern Caribbean & Perfect Day from Port Canaveral on Star of the Seas — both call at Perfect Day at CocoCay, Royal Caribbean's private destination in the Bahamas (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/star-of-the-seas/things-to-do).
- **Perfect Day at CocoCay:** an award-winning private island destination in the Bahamas, exclusive to RC guests, with no separate admission fee. Free features include Oasis Lagoon (billed as the Caribbean's largest freshwater pool), five complimentary island dining spots, Splashaway Bay aqua park, Captain Jill's Galleon, and multiple beaches (South Beach, Chill Island, Cove Beach, Harbor Beach, Breezy Bay). Paid add-ons include Thrill Waterpark (14 waterslides, including Daredevil's Peak at 135 feet, billed as the tallest slide in the Caribbean), a 1,600-foot zip line, the "Up, Up & Away" helium balloon (rising to 450 feet), the adults-only Hideaway Beach, and Coco Beach Club overwater cabanas (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cococay-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/how-much-does-perfect-day-at-cococay-cost, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/guides/8-hours-on-royal-caribbeans-perfect-day-at-cococay).

### Mediterranean
- **Home ports:** Rome (Civitavecchia) and Barcelona are the principal official home ports; the site also references Venice, Ravenna, Trieste, Athens and Haifa for Mediterranean/Adriatic/Greek-isles sailings (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/rome-italy, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/mediterranean-cruises).
- **Season:** best time to visit is stated as spring (May–June) or autumn (September–October), as summer (July–August) brings peak heat (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/mediterranean-cruises).
- **Representative itinerary:** 7-Night Western Mediterranean cruise from Rome (Civitavecchia) on Legend of the Seas, calling at Naples/Capri (Italy), Barcelona and Palma de Mallorca (Spain), Provence/Marseille (France), and Florence/Pisa via La Spezia (Italy) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/itinerary/7-night-western-mediterranean-cruise-from-rome-civitavecchia-on-legend-LE7IP206).
- Legend of the Seas is officially confirmed sailing weeklong Mediterranean itineraries from Europe from Summer 2026 (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas).

### Northern Europe / Baltic / Norwegian Fjords
- **Home ports:** Southampton (England), Copenhagen (Denmark) and Amsterdam (Netherlands) are named as official departure ports for this region (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/northern-europe-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/copenhagen-denmark).
- **Season:** the site states early summer through early fall offers the best weather and long daylight hours (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/northern-europe-cruises).
- **Representative itineraries:** 9-night "Ultimate Norwegian Fjords" cruise calling at Copenhagen and Aarhus (Denmark), Oslo, Kristiansand, Haugesund, Olden, Ålesund and Bergen (Norway), and Amsterdam (Netherlands) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruises/itinerary/9-night-ultimate-norwegian-fjords-from-amsterdam-on-serenade/SR09V002); a shorter 7-night Norwegian Fjords cruise from Copenhagen is also offered (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruises/itinerary/7-night-norwegian-fjords-from-copenhagen-on-jewel/JW07CPH-585364309). Itinerary lengths in the region commonly run 5, 7, 8 and 9 nights (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/northern-europe-cruises).

### Alaska
- **Home ports:** Seattle, Washington and Vancouver, British Columbia (round-trip Inside Passage cruises); Seward, Alaska is used for one-way cruisetours combined with land touring (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/alaska-cruises).
- **Season:** summer months, with the site noting nearly 20 hours of daylight around 21 June and darker late-summer skies suiting Northern Lights viewing (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/alaska-cruises).
- **Representative itineraries:** 7-night Alaska Inside Passage cruise from Seattle on Voyager of the Seas, calling at Juneau, Skagway, Endicott Arm & Dawes Glacier, and Victoria (British Columbia) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/itinerary/7-night-alaska-adventure-cruise-from-seattle-on-voyager-VY07A426); 7-night northbound Alaska & Hubbard Glacier cruise from Vancouver on Ovation of the Seas calling at Ketchikan (Ward Cove), Sitka, Skagway, Hubbard Glacier and Seward (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/itinerary/7-night-northbound-alaska-hubbard-glacier-from-vancouver-on-ovation-OV07A421). Cruisetours combining a 2–6 night land tour with the 7-night cruise typically run 9–13 nights total (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/alaska-cruises).

### Asia (Singapore homeport, etc.)
- **Home ports:** Singapore is the principal official Asia home port; the destination overview also references Shanghai (Baoshan) and Tianjin, China as embarkation points (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/asia-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/singapore).
- **Ships:** Quantum of the Seas, Ovation of the Seas, and Navigator of the Seas are named as sailing Southeast Asia itineraries from Singapore (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/asia-cruises and related official itinerary/search pages).
- **Representative itineraries:** 5-night "Spice of Southeast Asia" cruise from Singapore, calling at Penang, Malaysia and returning to Singapore (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruises/itinerary/5-night-spice-of-southeast-asia-from-singapore-on-anthem/AN05I943); 12-night Singapore-to-Tokyo repositioning cruise on Navigator of the Seas calling at Hong Kong, Taipei, and Japanese ports including Nagasaki, Kumamoto and Kagoshima before Tokyo (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/itinerary/12-night-singapore-to-tokyo-cruise-from-singapore-on-navigator-NV12I202). Itinerary lengths from Singapore commonly run 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 nights (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/asia-cruises).

### Australia
- **Home ports:** Sydney and Brisbane (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/aus/en/cruise-ships/cruise-schedule/australia-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/sydney-australia, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/brisbane-australia).
- **Ships and season:** Anthem of the Seas returns to Sydney and Voyager of the Seas returns to Brisbane for the 2027/28 Australian summer season (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/aus/en/cruise-ships/cruise-schedule/australia-cruises).
- **Representative itineraries:** Anthem of the Seas offers 3–12 night itineraries from Sydney to destinations including Queensland, the South Pacific, Vanuatu, Fiji and New Caledonia; Voyager of the Seas offers 2–8 night itineraries from Brisbane including short weekenders and New Zealand sailings. A 17-night transpacific repositioning cruise from Honolulu to Sydney via French Polynesia (Papeete, Moorea, Raiatea), and a 25-night Seattle-to-Brisbane repositioning cruise via Honolulu and French Polynesia, are also offered (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/aus/en/cruise-ships/cruise-schedule/australia-cruises). A new private destination, "Lelepa" (Royal Beach Club Lelepa), is stated as debuting in 2027 as RC's first private destination in the Southern Hemisphere (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/aus/en/cruise-ships/cruise-schedule/australia-cruises).

### Middle East / Arabian Gulf (relevant to GCC agents)
Royal Caribbean **does have official, current coverage of Dubai and the wider Arabian Gulf** — this is not a fabrication and is confirmed across several official pages:
- Official destination pages exist for "Cruises from Dubai, United Arab Emirates," "Cruises to Dubai, United Arab Emirates," and "Cruises to Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/dubai-united-arab-emirates, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-to/dubai-united-arab-emirates, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-to/abu-dhabi-united-arab-emirates).
- The official "Arabian Gulf and North Africa Cruises" page confirms **Dubai (DXB) as a home port**, with itineraries calling at Suez Canal (Egypt), Muscat and Salalah and Khasab (Oman), Doha (Qatar), Bahrain, Sir Bani Yas and Abu Dhabi (United Arab Emirates), and Aqaba (Jordan) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/arabian-gulf-north-africa-cruises).
- The page states winter is recommended for the region ("Winter is one of the best times to visit the Arabian Gulf with a cruise, since it's still warm and dry here while it's cold and rainy in other parts of the world"), with summer months bringing extreme heat (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/arabian-gulf-north-africa-cruises). **UNVERIFIED**: exact season month range (e.g. "November–April") is not spelled out on the official page found — only the qualitative "winter" recommendation is confirmed.
- A specific official itinerary confirms a **15-night "Ultimate India, Sri Lanka & Malaysia" cruise departing Dubai on Serenade of the Seas** (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/gbr/en/cruises/itinerary/15-night-ultimate-india-sri-lanka-malaysia-from-dubai-on-serenade/SR15V001). Full port-by-port sequence and exact season for this sailing could not be extracted from the fetched page and should be re-confirmed on royalcaribbean.com at time of quoting to a client — **UNVERIFIED beyond ship name, home port, region names and night count**.
- The Dubai cruise terminal is **Port Rashid (Mina Rashid)**, Passenger Terminal 2/3, per the official FAQ giving driving directions from Dubai International Airport (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/driving-directions-port-dubai-united-arab-emirates). No on-site parking is available at this terminal (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/driving-directions-port-dubai-united-arab-emirates).
- **UNVERIFIED**: which specific ship(s) are deployed to Dubai/Abu Dhabi for the 2026/27 season specifically, and exact current-season night-length options beyond the one confirmed 15-night Serenade of the Seas sailing above — this could not be confirmed from the official pages accessed and should be checked directly on royalcaribbean.com or with RC's trade desk before quoting specifics to clients.

## 3. Positioning & clientele

- **Brand history/scale:** "Royal Caribbean has been delivering innovation at sea since its launch in 1969," describing itself as operating a global team of more than 50,000 crew delivering cruises to "more than 240 destinations" for "100 million+ guests" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us).
- **Biggest-ships-in-the-world positioning:** the official About Us page states plainly: "Icon of the Seas (2024) and Star of the Seas (2025) are not just the biggest cruise ships in the world, they're designed as all-in-one vacations" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us). The Oasis Class is separately billed as "World's Largest Cruise Ships" on its own official page (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/largest-ships-oasis-class).
- **Family/active-lifestyle positioning:** Icon of the Seas is described as "the world's leading sports cruise, made for sports lovers," and Star of the Seas is marketed as "The World's Best Family Vacation" (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/star-of-the-seas). The brand's own recognition list includes "Best Cruise Line Overall," "Best Entertainment" and "Best Private Island" accolades for 2025 (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/about-us).
- **Onboard USPs emphasised on official ship-feature pages:**
  - **FlowRider** — a complimentary 40-foot surf simulator using 30,000 gallons of water per minute, included in the cruise fare (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/voyager-of-the-seas/things-to-do/flowrider).
  - **Rock climbing wall** — a complimentary 40-foot wall (Deck 13 on relevant ships), open to guests as young as six, no reservation required (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/guides/11-things-to-do-adventure-of-the-seas).
  - **Ice skating** — Voyager of the Seas introduced Studio B, the fleet's first ice rink at sea, alongside the Rock Climbing Wall and Royal Promenade; complimentary skating and rentals are offered fleet-wide where fitted. Icon of the Seas features "Absolute Zero," billed as the biggest ice arena in the fleet (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/voyager-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas/things-to-do).
  - **Waterparks/waterslides** — Category 6 (Icon Class) is billed as the largest waterpark at sea; Perfect Day at CocoCay's Thrill Waterpark is billed as having the tallest slide in the Caribbean (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/how-much-does-perfect-day-at-cococay-cost).
  - **Neighbourhood concept** — Oasis Class and Icon Class ships are built around distinct "neighbourhoods" (e.g. Central Park, AquaDome, Thrill Island, Royal Promenade), each with its own dining, entertainment and activity identity (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/largest-ships-oasis-class, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas).
  - **Crown's Edge** (Icon Class) — described as a "skywalk, part ropes course, part thrill ride" experience (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/icon-of-the-seas).

## 4. Notes for GCC agents (fly-cruise hub information)

Based purely on official RC embarkation-port pages, not assumptions:

- **Direct regional relevance — Dubai/Arabian Gulf:** Royal Caribbean maintains official "cruise from Dubai" and "cruise to Dubai/Abu Dhabi" pages and an "Arabian Gulf and North Africa" itinerary programme homeporting in Dubai (Port Rashid/Mina Rashid terminal), with port calls across Oman, Qatar, Bahrain, the UAE (Abu Dhabi, Sir Bani Yas) and Jordan (Aqaba) (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/arabian-gulf-north-africa-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/driving-directions-port-dubai-united-arab-emirates). This means GCC-based clients can be offered a **regional, no-fly (or short-fly) embarkation option** directly from home territory. Agents should verify current-season sailing dates and ship assignment directly on royalcaribbean.com or via RC's trade contacts, since exact 2026/27 season deployment could not be fully confirmed from the pages accessible during this research (see Section 2 above).
- **Long-haul, fly-cruise itinerary linking the Gulf to Asia:** the official 15-night "Ultimate India, Sri Lanka & Malaysia" cruise departs from Dubai on Serenade of the Seas, giving a positioning-cruise option that bridges the Gulf and South/Southeast Asia in one sailing (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/gbr/en/cruises/itinerary/15-night-ultimate-india-sri-lanka-malaysia-from-dubai-on-serenade/SR15V001).
- **Mediterranean fly-cruise hubs:** for GCC travellers wanting the Icon Class or Oasis Class big-ship experience in Europe, the relevant official embarkation ports are **Rome (Civitavecchia)** and **Barcelona**, both well served by direct or one-stop flights from GCC hubs (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/rome-italy, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/mediterranean-cruises). Legend of the Seas (Icon Class) is confirmed sailing from these ports in Summer 2026 (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-ships/legend-of-the-seas).
- **Asia fly-cruise hub:** **Singapore** is RC's confirmed Asia home port, a common long-haul connection point for GCC travellers, with itineraries to Southeast Asia, Japan, Hong Kong and Taiwan (Source: Royal Caribbean official — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/asia-cruises, https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruise-from/singapore).
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether Royal Caribbean sells any additional GCC-region embarkation points beyond Dubai/Abu Dhabi (e.g. any Saudi Red Sea ports) — no official page confirming this was found during this research; agents should treat this as "no current official presence found beyond the Dubai/Arabian Gulf programme documented above" rather than assume it exists.
