## 1. Cruising from Dubai and the Gulf

### 1.1 What cruise terminals serve Dubai?

Dubai has two official cruise gateways:

- **Mina Rashid (Port Rashid)**, home to the **Hamdan bin Mohammed Cruise Terminal**, described by its operator as the world's largest single, covered cruise terminal facility. It can accommodate seven mega cruise vessels, or up to 27,000 passengers, simultaneously, and has operated as a homeport since 2007.
  (Source: DP World, Mina Rashid Port — https://www.dpworld.com/en/uae/ports-and-terminals/mina-rashid)

- **Dubai Harbour Cruise Terminal**, opened December 2021, comprising two passenger terminals with a combined floor area of c. 27,870 m², able to berth two of the largest cruise ships simultaneously, with combined annual capacity cited at over 1.2 million passengers.
  (Source: Dubai Harbour, official site — https://www.dubaiharbour.com/)

- The UAE's federal tourism ministry also lists a third emirate-level facility, the **Ras Al Khaimah Cruise Terminal**, alongside Dubai's Port Rashid and Abu Dhabi's Port Zayed, as the country's cruise gateways.
  (Source: UAE Ministry of Economy & Tourism, "Dubai Cruise Terminal" — https://www.moet.gov.ae/en/-/dubai-cruise-terminal)

**FAQ framing:** *"Which terminal will my ship use in Dubai?" — Dubai has two cruise gateways: Mina Rashid (Hamdan bin Mohammed Cruise Terminal) and Dubai Harbour Cruise Terminal. Your cruise line's booking confirmation will specify which one your ship uses, as both operate as active homeports.*

### 1.2 What about Abu Dhabi?

The **Abu Dhabi Cruise Terminal at Zayed Port** is operated by AD Ports Group. It comprises a 7,800 m² passenger hall with capacity for 5,000 passengers and berthing for three mega vessels simultaneously; a second terminal (Cruise Terminal 2) was added in Q4 2022.
(Source: AD Ports Group, Abu Dhabi Cruise Terminal — https://www.adports.ae/ports-terminals/cruise-terminals/abu-dhabi-cruise-terminal/)

Abu Dhabi also operates **the region's only dedicated cruise stopover beach**, at Sir Bani Yas Island in the Al Dhafra region — described officially as the first such facility in the Arabian Gulf.
(Source: Department of Culture and Tourism – Abu Dhabi, "Cruise Tourism" — https://dct.gov.ae/en/what.we.do/tourism/cruise.tourism.aspx)

### 1.3 What about Doha, Qatar?

Doha Port's **Grand Cruise Terminal**, opened in 2022, has dual terminals serving two berths, with capacity for up to 12,000 passengers per day, and full immigration, customs, duty free and tourist-information facilities on site, in coordination with Qatar Tourism, Immigration, Customs and Mwani Qatar.
(Source: Visit Qatar, "Doha Port" — https://visitqatar.com/intl-en/plan-your-trip/doha-port; Mwani Qatar, "Doha Port" — https://www.mwani.com.qa/English/Ports/Pages/DohaPort.aspx)

### 1.4 When does the Gulf cruise season run?

- Abu Dhabi's official cruise season is stated as **October to May**, comprising three itinerary types: (a) circular seasonal tours starting/ending in Abu Dhabi, (b) regional port calls within wider Arabian Gulf itineraries, and (c) round-the-world voyage calls.
  (Source: Department of Culture and Tourism – Abu Dhabi, "Cruise Tourism" — https://dct.gov.ae/en/what.we.do/tourism/cruise.tourism.aspx)
- Doha Port's cruise terminal sees peak activity **November to April**, aligning with the region's cooler winter months.
  (Source: Visit Qatar, "Doha Port" — https://visitqatar.com/intl-en/plan-your-trip/doha-port)

**FAQ framing:** *"When is the Gulf cruise season?" — The Gulf's winter cruise season broadly runs from October/November through April/May, coinciding with the region's cooler, more comfortable weather. Abu Dhabi's official season is stated as October–May; Doha Port's peak cruise activity runs November–April.*

## 2. Visas for cruise passengers

### 2.1 Do I need a visa to join a cruise from Dubai?

The UAE's Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs & Port Security (ICP) operates a dedicated **multiple-entry visa for tourism via cruise ships and leisure boats**, grantable where the applicant has a tourism itinerary and the sponsor/host is a licensed cruise/leisure-boat tourism establishment.
(Source: ICP UAE, media centre notice on visit-visa categories — https://icp.gov.ae/en/media-center/identity-citizenship-customs-and-port-security-adds-four-new-visit-visa-categories-and-updates-conditions-and-durations-of-existing-visas/)

Dubai's General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) separately lists a **"Multiple Entry Permit for Tourists arriving on cruise ships"**: valid for entry within 60 days of issuance, and for 30 days' multiple use after first entry, applied for by the tourist agent/sponsor, with online application, payment and electronic printing, and roughly 48-hour processing.
(Source: GDRFA Dubai, service page — https://www.gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/bdbc5a50-b739-11ed-5210-4cd98f768936)

**FAQ framing:** *"Do I need my own visa for a Dubai cruise?" — The UAE operates a dedicated multiple-entry cruise/leisure-boat tourism visa, arranged through your cruise line or agent as the licensed sponsor, rather than an individual visa application. Requirements can vary by nationality — always confirm at the time of booking.*

### 2.2 What about multi-country Gulf itineraries (UAE / Qatar / Bahrain / Oman)?

- **Qatar**: over 100 nationalities qualify for **visa-free entry**; GCC nationals (Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Saudi Arabia, UAE) enter without a visa. Travellers not covered can apply for an e-visa via the Hayya platform; passports must be valid at least three months from arrival.
  (Source: Visit Qatar, "Visas" — https://visitqatar.com/intl-en/plan-your-trip/visas; Hayya official portal — https://www.hayya.qa/)
- **Bahrain**: operates its own official e-visa system at **evisa.gov.bh**, which the Bahraini authorities state is the *only* official channel for Bahrain e-visas. We could not retrieve the live page during this research to confirm cruise-specific wording — agents should verify current eligibility lists directly on evisa.gov.bh before advising clients. **UNVERIFIED (page inaccessible at time of research; portal name/URL confirmed via UAE/Qatar cross-reference searches only)**.
  (Source to re-check: Bahrain eVisa, official portal — https://evisa.gov.bh/)
- **Oman**: operates an official e-visa system at **evisa.rop.gov.om** (Royal Oman Police), including an eligibility wizard that accounts for GCC residency status. Specific sea-port/cruise-passenger provisions were not confirmed in the sections we could access. **UNVERIFIED for cruise-specific detail — verify directly with evisa.rop.gov.om.**
  (Source: Royal Oman Police eVisa — https://evisa.rop.gov.om/visa-eligibility)

**FAQ framing:** *"My cruise stops in more than one Gulf country — do I need separate visas?" — Requirements vary by nationality and by country. The UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman each run their own official visa/e-visa systems, and GCC nationals generally move visa-free between GCC states. Because rules differ per passport and can change, always confirm your specific requirement per itinerary and per nationality directly with Byond Borders or your cruise line before booking.*

### 2.3 What about Mediterranean/Schengen cruises?

The EU's official Schengen visa guidance confirms a Schengen visa permits stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period; applications should be lodged 15 days to 6 months ahead of travel, with a valid passport (3+ months' validity beyond the stay), travel medical insurance, and supporting documents. Where a cruise calls at several Schengen countries, applicants should apply via the consulate of the country where they will spend the most time, or their first port of call if time is evenly split.
(Source: European Commission, Migration and Home Affairs — "Applying for a Schengen visa" — https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/visa-policy/applying-schengen-visa_en)

**FAQ framing:** *"Do I need a Schengen visa for a Mediterranean cruise?" — If you require a Schengen visa and your cruise calls at multiple Schengen countries, apply through the consulate of the country where you'll spend the most time (or your first port of call, if time is equal). Visa need depends on your nationality — check per booking.*

### 2.4 General visa framing for the FAQ

*"Visa requirements vary by nationality, cruise itinerary and country of call. Because rules can change and differ passport-to-passport, please confirm your specific visa requirement with Byond Borders or your travel agent at the time of booking, rather than relying on general guidance."*

## 3. What's included in a cruise fare, dining & drinks packages, and kids' programmes

*(Generic, cross-line facts drawn from official cruise-line sites; not Gulf-specific.)*

### 3.1 What does a standard cruise fare include?

- **Royal Caribbean**: cruise fare includes the stateroom, complimentary dining across multiple venues, entertainment (including Broadway-style shows, ice shows and AquaTheater performances on select ships, live music, comedy and themed parties), and activities/access to private destinations. **Not** included: soda, wine, beer, spirits, cocktails, premium coffee/tea, bottled water and fresh-squeezed juices.
  (Source: Royal Caribbean, "What's Included on a Cruise" — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/guides/what-is-included-on-a-cruise)
- **MSC Cruises**: fare includes the main dining room, buffet, and continental-breakfast room service, plus pools, hot tubs, water park and sports-deck amenities, fitness facilities, and entertainment such as theatre shows, comedy and nightclub access on most ships. Basic non-alcoholic drinks (still water in the dining venues, iced tea, lemonade, coffee/tea, juice at breakfast) are included; specialty dining and most drinks packages are paid extras.
  (Source: MSC Cruises, on-board dining pages — https://www.msccruisesusa.com/on-board/dining-drinks)

**FAQ framing:** *"What's included in my cruise fare?" — Typically: your cabin, main-restaurant and buffet dining, standard non-alcoholic drinks at meals, and included entertainment and activities. Specialty restaurants, alcoholic drinks, premium coffee, bottled water, spa treatments and shore excursions are usually paid extras — check your specific line and fare type, as inclusions vary.*

### 3.2 Drinks packages

MSC Cruises' current core packages (per its official site) are the **Premium Extra**, **Alcohol-Free**, and **Minors** packages (the previous "Easy"/"Easy Plus" tiers were retired). The Premium Extra package covers cocktails, premium spirits, wine/champagne by the glass, draft/bottled beer, specialty coffee, and soft drinks, and is included automatically with the MSC Yacht Club experience.
(Source: MSC Cruises, "Drinks Packages and Beverage" — https://www.msccruisesusa.com/on-board/dining-drinks/drinks-packages)

**FAQ framing:** *"Should I buy a drinks package?" — Most lines offer tiered drinks packages (alcoholic, alcohol-free, and specialty-coffee/soft-drink options) as a paid add-on to the base fare. What's covered — and whether it's worth it — depends on the line and package tier, so compare the official package terms for your specific sailing.*

### 3.3 Kids' programmes

Royal Caribbean's **Adventure Ocean®** youth programme (ages 3–11, split into Aquanauts 3–5, Explorers 6–8, Voyagers 9–11) and its **Teens Club** (ages 12–14 and 15–17) are included in the cruise fare at no extra charge; the **Royal Babies and Tots℠ Nursery** for younger children is a paid, hourly service.
(Source: Royal Caribbean, "Best Cruises for Kids and Teens" — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/experience/cruises-for-kids-and-teens; Royal Caribbean FAQ, "What activities are available for children and teens?" — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/children-teen-activities)

**FAQ framing:** *"Are kids' clubs included in the cruise price?" — On most major lines, supervised kids' and teens' clubs are included in the cruise fare; specialist nursery care for the very youngest children is usually a separate paid, hourly service. Age bands and exact inclusions vary by line — check the specific ship and sailing.*

## 4. Halal and dietary provision at sea

**Source-policy note:** we only name a line here where we could verify the claim directly on that line's own official domain. Several lines are widely reported (via aggregator/travel-press sites) to offer halal provision, but we could not independently confirm this on their official sites during this research (MSC Cruises' and Costa Cruises' own domains blocked automated access throughout this research) — those are marked UNVERIFIED and should not be published as confirmed facts without the client re-checking directly.

### 4.1 Confirmed via official source: Royal Caribbean

Royal Caribbean's official FAQ states: **"We offer Halal meat on sailings departing from Dubai and Singapore only."** This is halal-prepared beef or chicken served in the main dining room — the line is explicit that it offers halal **meat**, not complete halal meals. Requests must be submitted via the Special Needs form **90 days before departure**.
(Source: Royal Caribbean, official FAQ — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/aus/en/faq/questions/can-we-offer-halal-meals)

Royal Caribbean's broader dietary-accommodation FAQ separately confirms it caters for food allergies, gluten-free, kosher, low-fat/low-sodium, vegetarian (available daily without request), vegan (on request) and lactose-free/soy-milk needs, requested via **special_needs@rccl.com** at least 45 days before sailing (90 days for European/South American itineraries; kosher-for-Passover also needs 90 days' notice).
(Source: Royal Caribbean, official FAQ — https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/dining-dietary-restrictions-customer-care)

**FAQ framing:** *"Is halal food available on board?" — Royal Caribbean officially confirms halal meat (not a full halal menu) in the main dining room on sailings departing Dubai and Singapore, requested at least 90 days ahead via their Special Needs process. Provision varies significantly by cruise line — always confirm directly with the specific line for your sailing before promising halal availability to a client.*

## 5. Best time to cruise the Gulf, and cruise-and-stay basics

### 5.1 Best time to cruise the Gulf

- Dubai's official tourism board states the best time to visit is **November to March**, with the coolest months being **January–February** (daytime highs around 25°C, overnight lows around 15°C); this is also the city's peak tourist season.
  (Source: Visit Dubai, "Weather in Dubai" — https://www.visitdubai.com/en/plan-your-trip/weather-in-dubai)
- Abu Dhabi's official cruise season runs **October to May** (see §1.4).
  (Source: Department of Culture and Tourism – Abu Dhabi — https://dct.gov.ae/en/what.we.do/tourism/cruise.tourism.aspx)
- Doha Port's peak cruise activity runs **November to April** (see §1.4).
  (Source: Visit Qatar — https://visitqatar.com/intl-en/plan-your-trip/doha-port)

**FAQ framing:** *"When's the best time to cruise the Gulf?" — The Gulf's cruise season runs broadly October/November through April/May, taking advantage of cooler, more comfortable weather region-wide. Peak comfort (and peak demand) is typically December to February.*

### 5.2 Cruise-and-stay basics

Abu Dhabi's official tourism authority frames its cruise programme around three models — circular seasonal tours (start/end in Abu Dhabi), regional Gulf itineraries (Abu Dhabi as one call among several), and round-the-world voyage calls — which is the official basis for building pre- and post-cruise stay add-ons around a homeport call.
(Source: Department of Culture and Tourism – Abu Dhabi, "Cruise Tourism" — https://dct.gov.ae/en/what.we.do/tourism/cruise.tourism.aspx)

**FAQ framing:** *"Can I add hotel nights before or after my cruise?" — Yes — Gulf homeports such as Dubai and Abu Dhabi are well set up for cruise-and-stay itineraries, since both cities operate as circular-tour homeports (guests embark and disembark there) as well as regional and world-voyage calls. Talk to Byond Borders about combining your cruise with pre- or post-cruise hotel nights in the homeport city.*
